Asking Clarification Questions

For teacher reference only — not shown to students · matches the interactive Classwork file

How to use this key: Items in Transform It and Fix My Mistake are auto-checked in the student file, but accept several valid phrasings — this key lists all accepted answers. Items in Reading, Speaking, and Wrap-Up are open production tasks with no single correct answer — the answers below are model examples to guide your live correction, not the only acceptable response.
2
Transform It — Answer Key
1
"What time does the meeting start?"
Could you tell me what time the meeting starts?
Do you know what time the meeting starts?
Wh- embedded question — statement word order, no "does."
2
"Is the report finished?"
Could you tell me if the report is finished?
Could you tell me whether the report is finished?
I'm not sure whether the report is finished.
I'm not sure if the report is finished.
Yes/No questions embed with "if" or "whether," not a question word.
3
"What does 'ASAP' mean in this context?"
Could you clarify what 'ASAP' means in this context?
Do you know what 'ASAP' means in this context?
No "does" inside the embedded clause — "what ASAP means," not "what does ASAP mean."
4
"Did Grace send the invoice?"
I was wondering if Grace sent the invoice.
I was wondering whether Grace sent the invoice.
Do you know whether Grace sent the invoice?
Do you know if Grace sent the invoice?
Past simple "did" disappears — the embedded verb becomes "sent."
5
"Where is the file saved?"
Could you tell me where the file is saved?
Would you know where the file is saved?
Statement word order: "...where the file is saved," not "...where is the file saved."
6
"Are we presenting on Friday?"
Can you confirm if we're presenting on Friday?
Can you confirm whether we're presenting on Friday?
I'm not sure whether we're presenting on Friday.
I'm not sure if we're presenting on Friday.
"Confirm" pairs naturally with "if/whether" for Yes/No embedded questions.
3
Fix My Mistake — Answer Key
1
"Explain me the schedule."
Could you explain the schedule to me.
"Explain" needs "to" before the person.
2
"What means 'deliverable'?"
What does 'deliverable' mean?
Direct Wh- questions need "does": "What does X mean?"
3
"Can you tell me what is the password?"
Can you tell me what the password is?
Embedded questions use statement word order — no inversion.
4
"Could you tell me is the file ready?"
Could you tell me if the file is ready?
Yes/No embedded questions need "if" or "whether" — never just inverted word order.
5
"Clarify me the process, please."
Could you clarify the process for me, please.
Could you clarify the process, please.
"Clarify" doesn't take a direct object pronoun the way Spanish "aclárame" does — add "for me" or drop it.
4
Reading — Model Answers
Context
Grace's email: "...please send it over before it's too late." (no date given)
Wh- model: Could you tell me exactly when you need the numbers by?
If/whether model: Could you confirm if tomorrow morning works, or is there a specific deadline?
Any well-formed embedded question that actually asks for the missing date/time is acceptable. Check for two genuinely different trigger phrases, not the same one reworded.

Speaking — Sample Answers per Card
Card 1
Wei Zhang, Samsung — "Just send it whenever, but not too late."
Could you tell me exactly when you need it by?
Can you confirm if end of day works?
Card 2
Amara Chukwu, Coca-Cola — "Can you check the numbers before the call?"
Could you tell me what time the call starts?
Do you know if you need all the numbers or just this quarter's?
Card 3
Lucas Meyer, IKEA — "I need the file in the usual format."
Could you clarify what you mean by "the usual format"?
Do you know if that's the same format as last quarter?
Card 4
Isabela Duarte, Nike — "Let's touch base sometime this week."
Could you tell me which day works best for you?
Would you know if mornings or afternoons are better?
Any two grammatically correct embedded questions (one Wh-, one if/whether where applicable) that meaningfully request the missing information are acceptable.
6
Wrap-Up — What to Listen For
Fully open — students write one original clarification question about their own job.
Example of a strong answer: "Could you tell me if the client approved the new design yet?"
Check for: a real trigger phrase (not a bare direct question), correct word order in the embedded clause, and — if it's a Yes/No question — the presence of "if" or "whether."
